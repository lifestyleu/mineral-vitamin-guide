The Minerals & Vitamins Every Woman Should Know

Why “normal” labs don’t always mean you’re not deficient — and what your symptoms might actually be telling you.

Important note

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not intended to diagnose, treat, or replace medical care. Always work with your healthcare provider before making changes to your supplements or health routine.

Ever feel like you’re doing everything “right” — eating well, exercising, sleeping (sort of) — but your body still isn’t cooperating? Fatigue, mood swings, stubborn skin issues, or a cycle that feels off?

Bloodwork can come back “normal” and still miss the full picture. Minerals and vitamins don’t just need to be present in your body — they need to be balanced, absorbed, and actually usable by your cells. This guide walks through the most common nutrients we see out of balance in women, what it might feel like in your body, and simple ways to start supporting yourself.

Minerals

Magnesium

The mineral that runs 500+ processes in your body — and the one you’re most likely running low on.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Waking up between 2–4am and not being able to fall back asleep
  • PMS, cramps, or period pain that feels worse than it should
  • Muscle twitches, tension, or restless legs
  • Feeling anxious or “on edge” for no clear reason
  • Headaches or migraines that show up around your cycle

Quick tip

Leafy greens, pumpkin seeds, and dark chocolate are natural sources — and a warm Epsom salt bath before bed can help your body wind down.

Iodine

Not just a “thyroid thing” — it supports your metabolism, your cycle, and your breast and ovarian tissue.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Cold hands and feet, even when everyone else is warm
  • Feeling like your metabolism has slowed to a crawl
  • Heavy, irregular, or unpredictable periods
  • Hair thinning (especially the outer third of your eyebrows)
  • Brain fog or low motivation that doesn’t match your effort

Quick tip

Seafood and seaweed are the richest natural sources — sea salt does not contain iodine the way iodized salt does, so don’t assume you’re covered just because you use “good” salt.

Zinc

Your skin, your immune system, and your hormones all lean on this one more than people realize.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • White spots on your nails
  • Acne or breakouts that won’t clear up
  • Catching every cold or bug that goes around
  • Slow healing (cuts, breakouts, etc. take longer than they should)
  • Hair shedding more than usual

Quick tip

Pumpkin seeds, oysters, and grass-fed beef are strong sources — and if you’re also low in magnesium, know that the two often show up together.

Copper (the tricky one)

Copper isn’t just “high” or “low” — it’s about whether your body can actually use the copper it has. This is a mineral where balance matters more than the number.

If your copper is dysregulated, it might show up as:

  • Anxiety, racing thoughts, or feeling wired for no reason
  • PMDD or estrogen-dominant symptoms (heavy periods, tender breasts, mood swings)
  • Migraines, especially hormonal ones
  • Skin issues like acne or rashes
  • Feeling emotionally reactive or easily overwhelmed

Supporting bile flow & copper clearance

Copper is cleared through bile, so the goal is to keep bile moving. A few habits that help:

  • Digestive bitters before meals — a few drops of herbal bitters (gentian, dandelion root) about 10–15 minutes before eating stimulates bile production and flow
  • ACV in water before meals — a tablespoon of raw apple cider vinegar in water helps acidify the stomach and prime digestion, which supports bile release downstream
  • Bitter foods — arugula, radicchio, endive, celery, artichoke, and radish all naturally stimulate bile; adding these to meals is a low-effort daily habit
  • Lemon water in the morning — warm lemon water first thing is a gentle liver and bile stimulant to start the day
  • Stay regular — copper leaves the body through stool, so daily bowel movements matter; if you’re constipated, copper (and other toxins) can recirculate
  • Castor oil packs — applied over the liver area a few times a week, these are a traditional tool for supporting liver and bile duct function

Selenium

The mineral your thyroid needs to actually convert its hormone into a usable form.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Fatigue that sleep doesn’t fix
  • Hair loss
  • Getting sick often or slow to bounce back
  • Trouble conceiving or early pregnancy loss
  • Hypothyroid-type symptoms even when labs look “normal”

Quick tip

Brazil nuts are famous for a reason — just 1–2 a day can meaningfully support your selenium levels.

Calcium (in balance, not just intake)

Calcium has a calming, sedating effect on your nervous system — helpful in the right amount, but a problem when it builds up as a stress response.

If it’s out of balance, it might show up as:

  • Feeling flat, numb, or disconnected from your emotions
  • A metabolism that feels “stuck” no matter what you try
  • Constipation or sluggish digestion
  • Feeling resistant to change, even when you want it
  • Low motivation or feeling stuck in freeze mode

Quick tip

This one is less about eating more calcium-rich foods and more about supporting your stress response — magnesium-rich foods and nervous-system downregulation (breathing, rest, less overexertion) matter more here.

Boron

An underrated mineral for hormone production, bone health, and easing menopause symptoms.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Hot flashes
  • Vaginal dryness
  • Joint stiffness or achiness
  • Brain fog or trouble with mental sharpness
  • Bone density concerns

Quick tip

Found in fruit, avocados, and beans — an easy one to boost through food alone.

Sodium

Regulated by your adrenal glands — so sodium issues are often a stress issue in disguise.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Feeling wiped out after a normal workout
  • Low blood pressure or dizziness when standing up
  • Low mood or apathy
  • Bloating (often from low stomach acid, not too much salt)
  • General fatigue that doesn’t match your effort level

Quick tip

If you’re low here, this usually isn’t about cutting salt more — it’s often the opposite. Adding a pinch of sea salt to water in the morning is a simple starting point.

Iron

The most common deficiency in women of reproductive age — often missed because “normal” ranges are wide.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Fatigue that hits especially hard
  • Pale skin, brittle nails, or hair shedding
  • Shortness of breath with mild activity
  • Cravings for ice or non-food items (a classic but often missed sign)
  • Restless legs
  • Heavy periods (which can also be the cause, not just a symptom)

Quick tip

Pair iron-rich foods (red meat, spinach, lentils) with vitamin C to improve absorption — and avoid having coffee or tea right around iron-rich meals, since they block absorption.

Vitamins

Vitamin D

Not just a vitamin — it acts more like a hormone in your body, and where you get it from actually matters.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Getting sick often or slow to recover
  • Low mood, especially in winter months
  • Fatigue that doesn’t improve with sleep
  • Bone or joint aches
  • Hair shedding
  • Trouble regulating your cycle or fertility challenges

Why sun ≠ supplement

Your skin only makes vitamin D from UVB rays, and UVB is only strong enough when the sun is high in the sky — generally within a couple hours of solar noon. Morning and evening sunlight feel great and support your circadian rhythm, but they won’t meaningfully raise your vitamin D. When your skin does make D from midday sun, it also produces it as a sulfated form that behaves differently in your bloodstream than the D3 you take in a capsule, and sun exposure triggers other beneficial effects (like nitric oxide release for blood flow) that a supplement can’t replicate. Oral D3 can still effectively raise your blood levels and is genuinely useful when midday sun isn’t realistic — but it’s not a perfect substitute for what your skin does under a high sun.

Tip: Aim for a few minutes of midday sun on bare skin (arms, legs) a few times a week when possible — no sunscreen for that short window. If you’re supplementing instead, pair D3 with vitamin K2 and magnesium so your body can actually use it properly.

Vitamin B12

Essential for energy, nerve function, and red blood cell production — and one of the easiest vitamins to be low in without knowing it, especially if digestion is off.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Fatigue that feels “bone deep”
  • Brain fog, poor memory, or trouble concentrating
  • Numbness or tingling in hands/feet
  • Pale skin or feeling short of breath easily
  • Mood changes, low motivation, or irritability
  • Tongue soreness or a smooth/shiny tongue

Quick tip

B12 depends on healthy stomach acid and gut function to absorb — so low stomach acid, gut infections, or long-term antacid/PPI use can cause a B12 issue even if you’re eating enough. Vegetarians and vegans are especially prone to low intake since B12 mainly comes from animal foods.

Vitamin B6

Needed for neurotransmitter production (mood!), hormone metabolism, and how your body processes protein.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • PMS or mood swings around your cycle
  • Low mood or irritability
  • Trouble sleeping
  • Nausea, especially with birth control use (it depletes B6)
  • Water retention or bloating
  • Cracking at the corners of the mouth

Quick tip

Oral contraceptives are one of the most common causes of B6 depletion in women — worth knowing if you’ve been on/off birth control and notice mood shifts.

Folate (B9)

Critical for cell division, mood, and detoxification — especially important pre-conception and during pregnancy.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Fatigue
  • Low mood or feeling “flat”
  • Trouble concentrating
  • Pale skin
  • Mouth sores
  • Concerns with fertility or early pregnancy

Quick tip

Look for “folate” rather than “folic acid” on labels when possible — folic acid is the synthetic form and some people don’t convert it efficiently due to a common genetic variation (MTHFR), meaning they need the active form (methylfolate) instead.

Vitamin A

Supports vision, skin, immune function, and — often overlooked — hormone production and thyroid conversion.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Dry skin or dry eyes
  • Frequent infections
  • Poor night vision
  • Acne that won’t resolve
  • Slow wound healing

Quick tip

True vitamin A (retinol) comes from animal sources like liver, egg yolks, and butter — beta-carotene from plants (carrots, sweet potatoes) has to be converted, and that conversion is inefficient for a lot of people, especially with thyroid issues.

Vitamin K2

Works as a partner to vitamin D and calcium — it directs calcium to your bones and teeth instead of your arteries and soft tissue.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Easy bruising
  • Concerns with bone density
  • Taking vitamin D but not feeling the benefits you’d expect
  • Calcium buildup concerns (arteries, joints)

Quick tip

Fermented foods (like natto or sauerkraut) and grass-fed dairy are natural sources — if you supplement with vitamin D, K2 is one of the most important nutrients to pair it with.

Omega-3s

Not a vitamin or mineral, but essential fats your body can’t make on its own — they show up constantly in hormone and mood conversations.

If you’re low, it might show up as:

  • Dry skin or brittle nails
  • Joint stiffness or achiness
  • Low mood or increased anxiety
  • Brain fog
  • Worse PMS or period pain (omega-3s help balance inflammatory hormones involved in cramping)

Quick tip

Fatty fish (salmon, sardines) a couple times a week is the most efficient source — plant sources like flax and chia contain a form your body has to convert, and that conversion is often inefficient.
